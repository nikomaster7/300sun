Ship shuttle

The bus your cruise line runs. It leaves from outside the terminal and drops you near the old town, often by the Trinitat bridge. Cruisers usually report around €10 return per person. Check the price and the last bus back with your ship.

About 25–30 min

Taxi rank

Official taxis at the terminal. They run on the meter. To the old town it’s usually a modest fare; cruisers often quote about €10 each way, more with traffic or on holidays. Ask the driver for “Torres de Serranos” or “Plaza de la Reina”.

About 15–20 min

City bus

The cheapest way. City buses run from outside the port into the centre; cruisers mention line 4 to the City Hall square, for about €1.50. Slower, and less handy on a tight port day.

About 30–40 min

Prices and times are what cruisers and locals usually see, not fixed fares. They change with traffic, holidays and your cruise line.

Planning your day

Work backwards from all-aboard.

  1. Take your all-aboard time, not the departure time in the brochure. That’s the one that matters.
  2. Be back at the terminal an hour before it. Allow 30 minutes for the ride back, more if it’s a holiday or a busy Saturday.
  3. What’s left is your time in town. On a 6–8 hour call that’s usually 3–5 hours: enough for the old town, the Central Market and lunch, without rushing.

Questions

What cruisers ask.

Can I walk from the Valencia cruise port to the city?

No. The cruise terminal is inside a large working port, 4–6 km from the old town, and the way out isn’t made for walking. Take a taxi, your ship’s shuttle or a city bus.

How long does it take to get from the cruise port to the old town?

About 15–20 minutes by taxi and 25–30 minutes by ship shuttle, more with traffic. The city bus takes longer.

How much is a taxi from the Valencia cruise port to the city centre?

Taxis run on the meter. Cruisers usually report around €10 each way to the old town, more with traffic or on holidays. If you join my walk, I book a driver I trust to pick you up.

Is the ship shuttle worth it?

It’s simple and drops you near the old town. Cruisers usually pay around €10 return per person. For two or more people a taxi often costs about the same and is faster, and you choose when to come back.

Can I see Valencia’s old town on a cruise stop?

Yes. Most ships stay 6–8 hours, which leaves 3–5 hours in town. That’s enough for the Cathedral area, La Lonja, the Central Market and a long lunch.

How early should I be back at the ship?

Aim to be at the terminal an hour before all-aboard, and leave the old town about 30 minutes before that. On a busy day, give yourself more.
